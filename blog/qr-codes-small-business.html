7 Ways I Actually Use QR Codes for My Small Business

By Ali · QR Studio Blog

A lot of "QR code marketing" advice online is written by people who have never actually run a small business. So here's a list without the fluff — just things I set up myself, why I needed them, and how long each one took.

1. A digital menu that never needs reprinting

Instead of paying for new printed menus every time a price changes, I made one QR code that points to a menu page. Customers scan it at the table, see the current menu, and I update prices whenever I want without touching a printer.

2. Payment links, without a card machine

For quick sales — a stall, a pop-up, a delivery — I generate a QR code that links straight to a payment page. No card reader needed, no waiting for a machine to connect.

3. Getting reviews without awkwardly asking

I put a small QR code near the counter that links directly to my Google review page. People scan it on their way out if they liked the service. It gets far more reviews than verbally asking ever did, because there's zero friction — no searching for the business name themselves.

4. Collecting WhatsApp orders

A code that opens a pre-filled WhatsApp chat ("Hi, I'd like to order...") saved me from customers typing out long messages. They scan, tap send, done.

5. Product info on packaging

For a few products I sell, I added a small QR code linking to ingredients, usage instructions, or a how-to video — information that wouldn't fit on a small label.

6. Event or appointment booking

A code on my business card that opens my booking calendar directly. People scan it right when we meet, instead of "I'll text you my number later" (which usually never happens).

7. Tracking which flyer actually worked

This one surprised me the most. Using a trackable QR code (not just a static one), I could see how many people actually scanned the flyers I put up in two different areas — and found out one location was getting almost zero scans, so I stopped wasting print money there.
