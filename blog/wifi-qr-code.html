How I Stopped Repeating My WiFi Password to Every Single Guest

By Ali · QR Studio Blog

My WiFi password is one of those long, ugly, auto-generated strings — a mix of capital letters, numbers, and at least one symbol I always mistype. Every time someone came over, the same thing happened: they'd ask for the password, I'd spell it out letter by letter, they'd type it wrong, I'd repeat it, and by the third try we were both a little annoyed. Family visits, friends staying over, even a technician who came to fix my AC — same routine, every time.

I finally fixed it with something so simple I felt silly for not doing it sooner: a WiFi QR code stuck near my router. Now anyone who wants to connect just opens their phone camera, points it at the code, and they're online in about two seconds. No typing, no spelling, no "was that a zero or the letter O?"

What a WiFi QR code actually is

It's not magic — it's just your network name and password encoded into a QR code in a specific format that phones already know how to read. When your camera scans it, your phone recognizes it as a WiFi network and shows a "Join" button. That's the entire experience for the person connecting.

How I made mine (takes under a minute)

  1. I opened QR Studio in my browser — no app install needed, it just works on the phone or laptop.
  2. Picked the "WiFi" QR type, typed in my network name and password.
  3. It generated the code instantly. I saved it and printed a small copy.
  4. Taped it to the inside of a kitchen cabinet, right where guests usually ask.

That's genuinely the whole process. No account, no signup, and it works completely offline once generated — nothing about your password gets sent anywhere, since the QR code is just a picture that your own phone reads locally.

Where else this has actually helped me

Once I made one for home, I started using the same trick everywhere:

  • A small QR code taped near my desk for when coworkers visit and need WiFi.
  • One printed on a card for guests staying over on Airbnb-style visits.
  • A version for my parents' house, since my dad kept calling me to ask for the password over the phone.

It's a small thing, but it removes a tiny recurring annoyance from daily life — and once you make one, you'll probably wonder why you didn't do it years ago.
